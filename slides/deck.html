Last-Layer Neurons Approximate High-Level Observables

In sections of their range, the network’s last-layer neurons are linear functions of standard jet observables, although the network only receives the particles’ (pT, η, φ). Neuron 12 falls linearly with the jet mass and reaches 0 near 90 GeV; neuron 2 rises linearly with the logarithm of the total pT above a threshold. Networks trained on particle-level inputs were found to learn quantities close to jet mass, N-subjettiness and energy correlations [2–4, 7, 8], and their decisions were mapped to energy-flow polynomials [5, 6].

Last-Layer Neurons Have a ReLU-Shaped Cutoff

Many of the network’s last-layer neurons are exactly 0 below a threshold of one observable and grow roughly linearly above it: the shape of a ReLU, max(0, a·(x − t)).

Left: network neuron 3 against x = Σᵢ pT,ᵢ ΔRᵢ / Σᵢ pT,ᵢ (260,000 test jets). Below x = 0.067, 97% of jets give exactly 0; one hinge explains R² = 0.77. Right: neurons 10 and 2 (JEDI-linear explorer page).

One observable alone does not describe most neurons. This suggests writing each neuron as a sum of such hinges in several observables, keeping the network’s last layer.

JEDI-linear

JEDI-linear is a graph-network jet tagger whose cost is linear in the number of particles, quantized for FPGA inference in the real-time trigger. Its inputs are the pT, Δη and Δφ of the 8 or 64 hardest particles of a jet; its outputs are scores for five classes: gluon (g), light quark (q), W, Z and top (t).

Five steps. Steps 1, 2 and 5 produce and test the formula; steps 3 and 4 are optional and only make it smaller.

Step 1: MARS, a Network Whose Units Read Observables

A network neuron is a ReLU of a weighted sum of ReLUs from the previous layer. A MARS neuron has the same form, but each inner ReLU acts on one physics observable minus a threshold, max(0, Q − t), which is an if-statement.

Neuron of the network

h = max(0,
    w₁·max(0, a₁·u + c₁)
  + w₂·max(0, a₂·u + c₂)
  + …
  + b)

u: all outputs of the layer before, themselves built from all particles’ (pT, η, φ). The inner units have no direct physical meaning.

Neuron written with MARS

h = max(0,
    β₁·max(0, Q₁ − t₁)
  + β₂·max(0, t₂ − Q₂)
  + β₃·max(0, Q₃ − t₃)·max(0, Q₄ − t₄)
  + … + β₀)

Q: physics observables of the jet; t: thresholds. max(0, Q − t) = “if Q > t: Q − t”. A product of two hinges is an if-statement with two conditions.

Each network neuron j is fitted separately: the MARS sum reproduces the neuron’s value before the ReLU, zⱼ, by least squares, and the neuron is max(0, ẑⱼ), as in the network.

8 · MARS: multivariate adaptive regression splines [1]; related: symbolic regression of network components [9]

Step 1: How MARS Adds Hinges

MARS starts from a constant and adds hinges one at a time. At each step it tries every observable and every threshold, keeps the hinge that reduces the squared error most, and refits all coefficients.

Example: network neuron 3 against x = ln(total pT of the jet), bin means. Dotted: candidate hinges at different thresholds. Solid: the least-squares best, max(0, a·(6.67 − x)), which follows the curve. The next hinge is chosen in the same way and added to this one.

Stopping: a hinge counts as useful if it raises the R² of the neuron value on validation jets by at least 10⁻⁴. The search stops after 5 steps in a row without such a gain, or at 100 hinges. In the 8-particle fit the 16 neurons ended with 18 to 100 hinges.

Candidates: all observables, thresholds at their 5–95% quantiles, and products of two hinges.

9 · illustration fitted to the neuron value; the formulas fit the value before the ReLU

Step 2: Joint Tuning of the Coefficients

Step 2 keeps the hinges and thresholds of step 1 and trains all coefficients of the 16 neurons together, through the network’s unchanged last layer, so that the class probabilities match a target, while each formula neuron is kept close to its network neuron.

stepwhat is fittedtargetloss
1 (MARS)each neuron separatelythe network’s neuron value before the ReLU, zⱼleast squares (regression)
2, tuned on the networkall coefficients togetherthe network’s output class probabilities for each jetcross-entropy to those probabilities + λR
2, tuned on truthall coefficients togetherthe true class of each jetcross-entropy to the true class + λR

R: mean squared difference between each formula neuron and the network’s neuron, in units of that neuron’s variance.

Without R, the 16 neurons are constrained only through the 5 class scores; tuning then changes them in directions that do not affect the class probabilities (backup slides). With λ = 0.01 agreement and accuracy are unchanged and every neuron stays close to the network’s.

10 · kept training step: highest validation agreement (network target) or accuracy (truth target)

Step 3 (Optional): Pruning

Makes the formula smaller; does not improve it

After step 2 the formula still has all the if-statements from step 1, and many of them add very little. Pruning removes those with the smallest contribution Iₘ (the typical amount they add), retrains the rest with the same loss (including λR), and keeps a cut if validation agreement drops by at most 0.1 points.

Step 4 (Optional): Further Simplification

Makes the formula smaller and easier to read; does not improve it

The pruned formula still has several if-statements on the same observable, and on observables that measure nearly the same thing (e.g. ΣzΔR² and λ₁). Step 4 removes more of them, including whole observables, and after each removal refits the remaining weights so that every neuron keeps the values it had in the pruned formula.

Target: the pruned formula’s own neuron values. The thresholds of step 1 are kept until the end, then refined and rounded. Jets whose class depends strongly on neuron j (near a decision boundary) get a larger weight wₙⱼ.

The size is the smallest that stays within a margin set before testing: 782 → 399 if-statements for the formula tuned on the network (within 0.5 points of validation agreement); 349 → 199 for the formula tuned on truth (within 0.4 points of validation accuracy). 8 particles.

A Neuron as Python Code

250 formula (tuned on the network, smaller version of the 690), 64 particles, file jedi_n64_formula250.py

Each neuron is a short Python function of the jet quantities Q, made only of if-statements and linear expressions.

def neuron_12(Q):
    z = -0.151
    if Q.girth2_top15 < 0.00586:
        z += 172.0 * Q.girth2_top15 - 1.00792
    if Q.mass < 59.7:
        z += -0.0234 * Q.mass + 4.21728
    if 59.7 <= Q.mass < 83.4:
        z += -0.119 * Q.mass + 9.9246
    if Q.sd_mass >= 124.0:
        z += 0.0144 * Q.sd_mass - 1.7856
    if Q.log_sum_pt < 6.85 and Q.lam2 < 0.0036:
        z += -3400.0 * (6.85 - Q.log_sum_pt) * (0.0036 - Q.lam2)
    if Q.mass < 90.0 and Q.psi_0p3 < 0.999:
        z += -3.47 * (90.0 - Q.mass) * (0.999 - Q.psi_0p3)
    if Q.sj3_pair_mass_max > 123.0 and Q.sj3_pair_mass_min < 65.0:
        z += 0.000437 * (Q.sj3_pair_mass_max - 123.0) * (65.0 - Q.sj3_pair_mass_min)
    return max(0.0, z)

  • The two jet-mass lines make z fall linearly from light jets to 0 at m = 83.4 GeV; above that they add nothing
  • The last if-statement uses the three subjets: it adds for jets with one subjet pair above 123 GeV and another below 65 GeV
  • return max(0.0, z) is the ReLU of the network neuron

399 formula (tuned on the network, smaller version of the 782), 8 particles

The if-statement ΣzΔR² > 0.0075 adds to neuron 10 only for wide jets, and the amount grows linearly with ΣzΔR² above the threshold.

Summary

  • The 16 neurons of the last hidden layer of the JEDI-linear jet tagger were replaced by 16 sums of if-statements on physics quantities of the jet; the network’s last layer was kept unchanged.
  • Method: MARS fit of each neuron to the network’s pre-activation, joint tuning of the coefficients through the last layer (to the network’s output, with each neuron kept close to the network’s), pruning, and simplification by distillation.
  • Tuned on the network, the formulas give the network’s class for 90.5% (8 particles, 782 if-statements) and 94.2% (64 particles, 690) of the 260,000 test jets, and their smaller versions for 90.1% (399) and 93.7% (250); accuracy is within 0.5 points of the network.
  • Each formula neuron corresponds to a physical property of the jet, for example ΣzΔR² and λ₁ (neuron 10) or pT concentration in the hardest particles (neuron 5). Like the network, the 64-particle formulas have windows at the W and Z masses, even when no mass observable is offered (through Σ zᵢzⱼΔRᵢⱼ² = (m/ΣpT)²).
  • For the same network with random weights, step 1 reaches a median R² of 0.55 and 0.16 per neuron (trained: 0.89 and 0.92), and the 8-particle formula uses mainly orientation-dependent observables (Δη or Δφ alone).
  • Jets that pass the same combination of if-statements form groups with distinct m, ΣzΔR² and pT sharing, and different class composition.

Backup: Why Tuned Formula Neurons Differ from the Network

Step 2 fits only the network’s class probabilities. The 16 neurons enter 5 class scores and the softmax ignores a common shift, so only 4 of the 16 neuron directions change the probabilities; changes along the other 12 cost nothing. In addition, the FPGA arithmetic wraps each neuron modulo 2^i (neuron 6: i = 4, values ≥ 16 wrap), which hides overshoots.

Deviation from the network neurons: 4.4 per jet after step 1, 16.9 after tuning, 88% of it along the 12 invisible directions (75% by chance); 4.0% of jets have a wrapped neuron. Adding λ Σⱼ (hⱼ − hⱼ,net)² / Var(hⱼ,net) to the step-2 loss with λ = 0.01: agreement 90.59% (λ = 0: 90.57%), accuracy 65.50% (65.49%), median neuron R² 0.84 (0.76), worst 0.63 (−18.9), wrapped 0.08%.
