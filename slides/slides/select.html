Step 1: How MARS Adds Hinges

MARS starts from a constant and adds hinges one at a time. At each step it tries every observable and every threshold, keeps the hinge that reduces the squared error most, and refits all coefficients.

Example: network neuron 3 against x = ln(total pT of the jet), bin means. Dotted: candidate hinges at different thresholds. Solid: the least-squares best, max(0, a·(6.67 − x)), which follows the curve. The next hinge is chosen in the same way and added to this one.

Stopping: a hinge counts as useful if it raises the R² of the neuron value on validation jets by at least 10⁻⁴. The search stops after 5 steps in a row without such a gain, or at 100 hinges. In the 8-particle fit the 16 neurons ended with 18 to 100 hinges.

Candidates: all observables, thresholds at their 5–95% quantiles, and products of two hinges.
