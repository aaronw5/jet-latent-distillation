Summary

  • The 16 neurons of the last hidden layer of the JEDI-linear jet tagger were replaced by 16 sums of if-statements on physics quantities of the jet; the network’s last layer was kept unchanged.
  • Method: MARS fit of each neuron to the network’s pre-activation, joint tuning of the coefficients through the last layer (to the network’s output, with each neuron kept close to the network’s), pruning, and simplification by distillation.
  • Tuned on the network, the formulas give the network’s class for 90.5% (8 particles, 782 if-statements) and 94.2% (64 particles, 690) of the 260,000 test jets, and their smaller versions for 90.1% (399) and 93.7% (250); accuracy is within 0.5 points of the network.
  • Each formula neuron corresponds to a physical property of the jet, for example ΣzΔR² and λ₁ (neuron 10) or pT concentration in the hardest particles (neuron 5). Like the network, the 64-particle formulas have windows at the W and Z masses, even when no mass observable is offered (through Σ zᵢzⱼΔRᵢⱼ² = (m/ΣpT)²).
  • For the same network with random weights, step 1 reaches a median R² of 0.55 and 0.16 per neuron (trained: 0.89 and 0.92), and the 8-particle formula uses mainly orientation-dependent observables (Δη or Δφ alone).
  • Jets that pass the same combination of if-statements form groups with distinct m, ΣzΔR² and pT sharing, and different class composition.
